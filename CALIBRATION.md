# Self-observed extrinsic calibration

Design doc for estimating the 6-DoF correction between the camera optical frame
*as TF declares it* and where the camera actually is — derived continuously from
the robot's own body passing through the field of view during ordinary operation.
No calibration target, no capture procedure, no operator. Companion to
[PLAN.md](PLAN.md) (the filter itself).

## Premise

The renderer already produces a dense, *noise-free*, geometrically known surface
in the camera frame on every frame where the robot is in view. Those frames occur
constantly during normal operation — that is the entire observation set. The
system's job is to extract a calibration from them as they arrive, and to be
honest about what it can't yet see.

The design constraint that follows: **we do not get to choose our observations.**
Everything below is shaped by that. No pose is requested, no hold is assumed, no
distance range is guaranteed. What the system controls is *which* observations it
keeps and *how much* it lets them move the estimate.

**Estimated:** one rigid `T_fix ∈ SE(3)`, applied to every link pose:
`T_cam_link_corrected = T_fix · T_cam_link_from_TF`. Optimized as a left
perturbation `ξ = (t, ω) ∈ se(3)`, `T_fix ← exp(ξ^)·T_fix`.

**Not estimated (v1):** intrinsics, distortion, depth bias, time offset, joint
offsets. Intrinsics are a fixed input (`camera_info`, optionally overridden) —
but see [Is it really extrinsic?](#is-it-really-extrinsic).

## Measurement model

For each usable pixel, the model side is a **plane** taken straight from the
rasterizer — the winning triangle's exact `n . X = d` in the camera frame — and
the measurement side is the **raw scalar depth**, never back-projected:

```
z_pred(xi) = d' / (n' . v),   n' = R(xi) n,   d' = d + n' . t(xi)
r_i(xi)    = z_meas - z_pred(xi)        ~  N(0, sigma(z)^2),  sigma(z) = a + b z^2
dr/dxi     = [ -n'^T , z_pred (n' x v)^T ] / (n' . v)
```

`v` is the pixel ray scaled so `v.z == 1`, so depth along it is exactly the z
coordinate. At `xi = 0` this reduces to `z_meas - z_render`.

**Why depth space rather than point-to-plane.** The two are the same information:
since the model point lies on the same ray as the measurement,
`n . (Q - P) = (z_meas - z_pred) . (n . v)`. But the point-to-plane form needs a
`(n . v)^2` factor in its variance to say what depth space says for free, and that
factor is easy to omit — as this implementation originally did, silently
mis-weighting every slanted surface by up to 25x. Working in the units the sensor
actually measures makes the noise model exact by construction. It also makes
`median_abs_residual_m` directly comparable to the median depth error reported in
[test/reachy_bag/README.md](test/reachy_bag/README.md).

**Why the plane comes from geometry, not from the depth image.** Rasterizing
analytic geometry to a depth image and then finite-differencing that image to
recover normals the geometry already had exactly is a lossy round trip, and it
costs twice. The normal becomes a numerical estimate, and `z_pred` inherits the
rasterizer's **linear-in-screen z** interpolation — an approximation even for a
plane, overestimating true depth by `(z1-z2)^2 / (2(z1+z2))`, a few mm on a face
spanning ~0.1 m at ~1 m. `IRenderer::render_planes()` emits `(n, d)` per pixel so
the estimator intersects the ray with the true plane instead. Measured: exact
planes cut the `t_x` error from 0.39 mm to 0.05 mm and tightened sigma by 26 %;
the finite-difference fallback carries a **-1.7 mm `t_z` bias**.

That fallback is not hypothetical — the EGL backend does not implement
`render_planes()` (it would need a second colour attachment), so the GPU path
takes it today and pays that bias. `Calibration.FallsBackToFiniteDifferencesWhen
PlanesAreUnavailable` pins both the fallback working and the size of the gap.

**A caution for synthetic tests.** Ground-truth depth must be generated by exact
ray-plane intersection, not by the rasterizer. Generating it with the rasterizer
makes both sides of the comparison wrong in the same way, which hid this entire
class of error until the plane path was added — and then made the *correct*
implementation look like a 1.5 mm regression.

### Why this form, given noisy depth

The obvious alternative — reproject the model point under the perturbation and
interpolate the measured depth at the new pixel — requires `∂D_meas/∂u`, the
**spatial gradient of the noisy measurement**. On a noisy ToF image that gradient
is dominated by noise and the linearization is invalid.

Here `z_meas` enters linearly and undifferentiated; every derivative comes from
the plane `(n, d)` and the ray `v`, which are exact. Zero-mean depth noise then averages out of
the normal equations instead of biasing them — which is what makes a purely
passive, no-averaging-step design viable at all. Point-to-plane rather than
along-ray is what supplies lateral observability: on a slanted link surface a
lateral shift produces an along-normal residual proportional to surface slope, so
silhouettes are not required.

The robust scale must be a MAD **about the median**, not `median(|e|)`. The
uncentred form conflates residuals spread wide because the sensor is noisy (a real
reason to deflate the frame's information) with residuals displaced together
because the estimate is currently wrong (a transient, and precisely the signal
being fitted). Uncentred, a 3σ systematic offset reads as scale ≈ 4.4, which both
widens the Tukey window and deflates the frame's information ~20×. In a batch
solve that only slows an early iteration; in the observer the deflated information
is committed permanently, so new evidence is suppressed exactly when the estimate
is most wrong. This was a real bug, found via the drift test.

**Noise handling is therefore statistical, not procedural.** An earlier draft of
this design averaged frames while the robot held still; that quietly assumed a
capture protocol. It is also unnecessary — M noisy frames of one pose contribute
the same information as one averaged frame, and the summation already happens in
the normal equations.

## Observation intake

Per frame, cheaply, in order:

1. **Is the robot visible?** Reuse `mesh_in_frustum` — no links in frustum, no
   observation. Free; the filter computes it anyway.
2. **Render and pair.** Reuse the filter's existing render of this frame. The
   calibrator adds no render cost in the common case.
3. **Per-pixel gates.**
   - Reject within ±k px (default 3) of a depth discontinuity **in the render** —
     the rim where flying pixels and mixed returns concentrate.
   - Reject high local roughness in the measurement (3×3 window) — a per-frame
     stand-in for the temporal-variance gate, since there is no temporal stack.
   - Reject grazing incidence (`|n_i·ray_i|` below threshold) — unreliable on
     every depth sensor.
   - Count occlusion: `Q_z < P_z − occl_margin` means a real object is in front of
     the robot at that pixel. One-sided, so never robustified — but the decision
     it feeds is a frame-level one, below.
4. **Weight** by the sensor noise model `σ(z) = a + b·z²` (`w ∝ 1/σ²`), times
   amplitude/confidence if the driver publishes it.
5. **Frame gates — the primary mechanism.** A frame is taken whole or not at all:
   - too few on-model samples (default < 150) → the robot is not meaningfully in
     view, so this is not an observation;
   - occluded fraction above threshold (default 15 %) → something real is standing
     in front of the robot;
   - usable fraction below threshold (default 40 % of on-model samples) → bad TF,
     mis-timed pose, or a surface that is all silhouette and grazing angle.

   Salvaging a partly-occluded frame looked attractive and is wrong: what survives
   is a spatially truncated, leverage-skewed slice of the robot, which biases the
   fit *while still reporting a tight sigma*. Measured on the synthetic bench, a
   heavily occluded stream fitted to 3.6 mm error while claiming σ ≈ 0.3–0.9 mm —
   confidently wrong, the one outcome this design is built to avoid. Dropping
   those frames costs nothing: passive operation supplies plenty of clean ones,
   and the reservoir below is bounded anyway.
6. **Decimate** to ~2000 surviving correspondences, stratified over the image, and
   hand them to the reservoir as a candidate.

No feedback loop: residuals are computed against the **raw measured depth**, never
against the filter's own masked output. The estimator consumes the filter's
render, not its decision.

### What the model does not capture

- **Pixel-to-pixel independence is assumed and is false.** Each frame's ~10k
  residuals are treated as independent; ToF multipath and finite rasterizer
  resolution correlate neighbours, so the effective sample count is lower and
  **reported sigma is optimistic on real data**. The synthetic bench cannot show
  this — its noise is i.i.d. by construction. *Measured in simulation against
  ground truth: sigma is optimistic by ~15x in the best case and by ~700x once a
  systematic mesh mismatch is present.* Sigma is a lower bound on random error, not
  an accuracy claim.
- **Everything on the prediction side is taken as exact** — meshes, TF, joint
  angles, intrinsics. All systematic, none modelled, so they appear as bias rather
  than variance. Hence the per-link residual breakdown and the intrinsics guard.
- **Depth scale/offset bias is unmodelled** and lands in `t_z`.
- **Association is assumed certain.** Projective association pairs pixels, not
  material points, which is exactly why the constraint is a plane and not a point:
  the tangential component carries no information and is left out.

## Streaming: a recursive SE(3) observer

Observations arrive forever and memory is bounded, so the estimator is recursive:
it keeps the estimate and a 6×6 information matrix, never the frames.

Each accepted frame contributes `dH = Σ wᵢJᵢᵀJᵢ` and `dg = Σ wᵢJᵢᵀrᵢ` — exactly the
quantities a batch solve accumulates — so batch and streaming agree; the observer
simply discards what it has already absorbed. Per-frame cost is one render (shared
with the filter's own), the residual pass, and a 6×6 solve.

**Left-invariant by construction.** The error is parameterised as a left
perturbation, `T_true = retract(ξ)·T̂`, matching the residual Jacobian. An update
`T̂ ← retract(δ)·T̂` then shifts the error coordinates by `−δ` and leaves the
covariance unchanged to first order, so no adjoint transport appears anywhere.
This is the natural choice here because the quantity being estimated is a fixed
mount error expressed in the camera frame.

**The prior stays outside the accumulator.** `H_prior` is re-evaluated against the
nominal every step rather than folded into the initial information. Folding it in
would let forgetting slowly erode the only thing holding never-observed directions
at nominal, and they would drift. Kept separate, a starved direction reports
`prior_dominance = 1.000` however long the observer runs.

**Forgetting.** `H_data ← H_data·exp(−dt/τ)` each step, so old evidence fades and
a mount that physically shifts is re-learned rather than outvoted by history.

**Step-change detection.** Forgetting alone is a bad trade: after `τ/dt` frames of
accumulated information a single frame can move the estimate by only ~`dt/τ`, so
tracking a knock requires a short `τ`, which throws away the averaging that makes
the estimate precise. Measured: with `τ` = 10 s at 10 Hz, a 30 mm shift was still
20 mm wrong after 200 frames.

A step change is distinguishable from noise, because it displaces a frame's
residuals *as a block* — which is `median |r|/σ`, uncentred. When that exceeds
`jump_sigma` (default 1.5) for `jump_frames` (default 4) consecutive frames, the
accumulated information is discarded and new evidence takes over at once. That
gives long-`τ` precision and fast re-learning together: on the bench, a 30 mm
shift is back under 1.2 mm within 40 frames at the default `τ` = 300 s, with no
false trigger across 60 healthy frames (converged `median |r|/σ` sits at 0.46,
against a threshold of 1.5). `jump_resets()` is exposed precisely because a
climbing count is the signature of the failure mode: a noise model wrong enough
that healthy frames look like step changes would reset continuously and never
accumulate precision.

**This subsumes the reservoir.** An earlier draft kept ~50 frames selected by
D-optimal information gain and re-solved over them. The information filter already
integrates *every* accepted frame in compressed form, so there is nothing to
select. What is genuinely lost is the ability to re-linearise old data after a
large estimate change — irrelevant at the scale of a mount error, and step-change
detection covers the one case where the estimate does move far. What is genuinely
gained is that a repetitive pose no longer needs de-duplicating logic; forgetting
bounds its influence instead. The remaining caveat is that a robot parked in one
pose accumulates real information from one geometry, so σ can become optimistic
about systematic error at that configuration — visible via `prior_dominance` and
the per-link residual breakdown, not silently.

## Solving: MAP with a prior at the URDF nominal

A passive system will spend its early life — possibly its whole life, if the robot
never moves through the right poses — with some DoF unobserved. A maximum-
likelihood solve would send those directions wherever the noise pushed them.

So estimate MAP, with a Gaussian prior centered on the URDF's declared extrinsic
(`ξ = 0`) and covariance set to the expected mounting error (default σ = 2 cm,
2°):

```
(H_data + H_prior) δξ = b_data − H_prior·ξ
```

Consequences, all of them desirable:

- Unobserved directions stay at the URDF value, automatically. No degeneracy
  blow-up, no "wait until conditioned" gate, no partial application of a coupled
  6-vector.
- The estimate is **always safe to apply**, from the first frame. It starts at
  nominal and moves only where evidence supports it.
- The prior encodes real knowledge: the mount error genuinely is small, and a
  solution 30 cm from nominal is far more likely to be a bad fit than a bad mount.

Outer loop re-renders to re-associate; inner loop runs IRLS Gauss-Newton with a
Tukey biweight, scale `σ = 1.4826·MAD(r)`, recomputed per outer iteration. Robust
loss is non-negotiable here: passive operation means people, objects, and clutter
are in frame constantly, and every pixel where the robot isn't actually visible is
a gross outlier.

Re-solves are triggered by reservoir change, amortized (default ≤ 1 Hz) on a
background thread. The 6-DoF problem over 50×2000 correspondences is small.

## Drift, restart, and staleness

- **Forgetting** and **step-change detection** are described above; together they
  cover slow drift and sudden knocks respectively.
- **Persistence.** `(T_fix, H_data)` should be written to a state file and reloaded
  at startup with inflated information — the robot does not re-learn from scratch
  every boot, but neither does it trust a months-old number absolutely. Not yet
  implemented.

## Introspection

There is no report artifact and no operator; the calibration is a running state of
the system. It exposes:

- A diagnostics topic: current `T_fix`, per-DoF σ from `(H_data+H_prior)⁻¹`,
  reservoir occupancy, inlier fraction, median residual, and **which DoFs are
  still prior-dominated** (`σ_posterior/σ_prior` near 1 ⇒ never observed).
- Per-link residual median/MAD. A single link fitting far worse than the others is
  a *mesh* problem, not an extrinsic one — which is exactly how the COLLADA
  node-transform bug in `test/reachy_bag/README.md` would have surfaced
  immediately instead of after a focal-length red herring. This stays a
  diagnostic, never a free parameter.
- Frame accounting: `frames_used / frames_total`. A collapsing ratio means the
  robot is chronically occluded or out of view, which is worth surfacing — the
  estimate silently ageing is not the same as the estimate being fine.
- Startup/periodic log line: the estimate and its uncertainty, in the same units
  the params file takes.

With intrinsics fixed, the degeneracies to expect are the ones a point-to-plane
residual inherits from the visible surface normals, not the classic focal/range
coupling: `t_x`, `t_y` and `ω_z` go weak when every visible surface is near
fronto-parallel (sliding the model in-plane changes no along-normal distance),
while `t_z`, `ω_x` and `ω_y` are fixed by a single flat surface. Confirmed on the
synthetic bench: fed nothing but a slab square-on to the camera, the estimator
recovers `t_z` to 0.3 mm while `t_x`, `t_y`, `ω_z` sit at nominal reporting
`prior_dominance = 1.000` — it says it learned nothing about them rather than
returning the noise.

### Is it really extrinsic?

With intrinsics frozen, a focal error is absorbed into `t_z` and a principal-point
error into `t_x/t_y`. Two cheap guards run alongside the solve:

1. **Radial residual test.** Regress per-pixel residual against radius from the
   principal point. A significant radial slope means the projection *scale* is
   wrong — focal error, not a rigid offset.
2. **Auxiliary 1-D fit.** Freeze extrinsics, fit `focal_scale` alone, report it.
   Outside ~[0.98, 1.02], warn: *"intrinsics suspect (focal_scale ≈ X); the
   extrinsic estimate is absorbing a projection-scale error — set
   `override_fx/fy`."*

Not hypothetical for this repo: the reachy ToF's recorded `camera_info` was ~30 %
off in FoV, and an extrinsics-only estimator pointed at that data would converge
confidently on a badly wrong `T_fix`.

## Where it lives

Inside the node, on a background thread — the calibration is a property of the
running filter, not an artifact someone generates. It reuses the frame's existing
render, so the per-frame cost is the gates plus decimation.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `include/depth_self_filter/calibration.hpp`, `src/calibration.cpp` | **ROS-free** estimator: frame gates, robust MAP/IRLS normal equations, batch `solve_extrinsic()` and streaming `ExtrinsicObserver`. Takes an `IRenderer`. |
| `src/depth_self_filter_node.cpp` | intake gates + decimation in `on_frame()`; apply `T_fix` in `build_items()` right after the TF lookup, so it is in force before frustum culling and rendering; publish diagnostics; persist state |
| `test/test_calibration.cpp` | synthetic recovery tests (below) |

No `IRenderer` change — normals are derived from the rendered depth buffer inside
the estimator. Keeping the estimator ROS-free matters for the same reason it does
for the renderers: the recovery tests must run in CI with no GPU, no bag, and no
ROS graph.

New parameters: `self_calibration_enable` (default **false** — opt-in until
validated on real data), `self_calib_prior_sigma_xyz/rpy`, `self_calib_reservoir`,
`self_calib_state_file`, and `extrinsic_correction_xyz_rpy` as a manual override
that also serves as the estimator's starting point.

## Validation

Implemented in `test/test_calibration.cpp` (marked ✅). All run in CI with no GPU,
no bag and no ROS graph, in ~0.6 s.

1. ✅ **Synthetic recovery.** Render at a ground-truth `T_fix*` over a stream of
   *unchoreographed* poses; corrupt with `σ(z) = 5 mm + 5 mm·z²` noise, flying
   pixels along silhouettes, 10 % dropouts, a background wall and an occluder.
   Asserts convergence within 3 mm / 0.2° **and** that reported σ brackets the
   true error — a calibrator that is confidently wrong is worse than one that
   stays at nominal. *Observed: ≤ 0.4 mm / 0.07°, every DoF inside 2σ.*
2. ✅ **Starvation.** Feed nothing but a surface square-on to the camera. Asserts
   `t_z` is recovered while `t_x`, `t_y`, `ω_z` stay at nominal and report
   themselves prior-dominated. *Observed: `t_z` to 0.3 mm; starved DoFs at
   ≤ 0.08 mm from nominal with `prior_dominance = 1.000`, against a true offset of
   15 mm they correctly refused to chase.*
3. ✅ **Frame rejection.** Three cases: occlusion on a third of the frames (those
   frames dropped, the rest still recover to 0.12 mm); every frame occluded
   (`frames_used = 0`, estimate untouched at nominal); robot swung out of the
   field of view for half the stream (`frames_used` exactly the in-view count).
4. ✅ **No-data and no-bias.** An empty stream leaves the estimate at nominal with
   every DoF pure prior; an already-calibrated stream does not manufacture an
   offset (*observed ≤ 0.63 mm / 0.05°*).
5. ✅ **Streaming convergence.** The observer, fed the same frames one at a time,
   reaches the same answer as the batch solve. *Observed: 0.27 mm from truth, and
   within 0.1 mm of the batch estimate over the same 60 frames.*
6. ✅ **Drift.** `T_fix*` changes mid-stream at the default `τ` = 300 s. Asserts the
   observer had settled on the first mount, that step-change detection fires, and
   that the new mount is re-learned. *Observed: 0.75 mm within 60 frames.*
7. ✅ **Streaming frame gates and starvation.** A fully occluded stream is refused
   frame by frame with the estimate untouched, then clean frames afterwards are
   absorbed normally; and a starved DoF stays at nominal indefinitely, confirming
   forgetting does not erode the prior anchor.
8. **Intrinsics-leak test** (milestone 4, now the top priority — a residual `cy`
   error is absorbed into exactly the `y`/`roll` pair that went degenerate above). Data rendered with a 15 % focal error;
   assert the radial-residual diagnostic fires.
9. ✅ **Real data.** Replayed against the Reachy2 ToF bag on an RTX 4090 (`egl`,
   exact planes) via `depth_self_calib_replay`. Median depth error 14.78 mm frozen
   at nominal — independently reproducing the +1.4 cm recorded in
   [test/reachy_bag/README.md](test/reachy_bag/README.md), which cross-validates
   the replay path — improving to 8.92 mm under free estimation. **The pose is
   nonetheless not trustworthy**: `corr(t_y, omega_x) = 0.992`, so the estimator
   slides along a near-degenerate valley to `y = +75 mm, roll = +7.9 deg`. Three
   bugs found and fixed, and one design lesson: `prior_dominance` alone is not a
   sufficient honesty signal — it reads 0.072 for a DoF that is barely observed
   once correlation is accounted for. The covariance eigenstructure must be
   reported. Full write-up in that README.

10. ✅ **Simulation, with ground truth.** Gazebo Reachy 2 (`reachy_sbc_experiment`,
   `sim.env`), head ToF at 640x480, `camera_info` exact by construction. The arm
   and neck are swept through diverse poses — the geometric diversity a passive
   bag cannot supply — and a KNOWN mount error is injected via
   `inject_offset_xyz_rpy`, so the correct answer is stated rather than guessed.

   | geometry | injected | recovered | median depth error |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | collision meshes | none | 10.8 mm / 1.5 deg **off** | 3.73 mm |
   | collision meshes | known | 10.69 mm / 1.605 deg off | 4.30 mm |
   | visual meshes | none | **0.15 mm / 0.03 deg** | 0.04 mm |
   | visual meshes | known | **0.20 mm / 0.055 deg** | 0.06 mm |

   **The estimator is correct.** Given geometry that matches what the sensor sees,
   it recovers a 26 mm / 2.7 deg mount error to 0.20 mm / 0.055 deg on a real robot
   model. Three things follow.

   - **The accuracy floor is mesh fidelity, not the estimator.** Gazebo renders
     the *visual* meshes; the filter renders *collision* meshes. That mismatch
     alone is the entire 10.8 mm bias, and it is present with and without
     injection — a fixed offset the estimator faithfully adds to the truth. On any
     real robot the same term appears wherever the collision hull departs from the
     surface the sensor actually sees.
   - **Motion diversity breaks the degeneracy.** `corr(t_y, omega_x)` falls from
     0.9922 on the bag to 0.79-0.83 under the sweep. It does not vanish, and
     `corr(t_x, omega_y) = -0.974` remains near-degenerate even so: a head camera
     looking down at its own arm sees a limited cone of geometry however it moves.
   - **Reported sigma is not an accuracy statement.** With collision meshes sigma
     read 0.016 mm while the true error was 10.8 mm — optimistic by a factor of
     ~700. Even in the best case it reads 0.013 mm against a true 0.20 mm. Sigma
     measures random noise under an independence assumption; systematic model error
     is invisible to it and dominates. Treat sigma as a lower bound and nothing
     more.

11. **Real data, unresolved** (milestone 4). Replay the reachy bag through the estimator (a
   test harness over the same ROS-free core — not a product surface). Current best
   is 85 % alignment / 1.4 cm median with a known ~5 px vertical residual; the
   estimator should absorb that residual passively, from the bag's ordinary teleop
   motion, with no staged poses.

Known and accepted: a systematic `t_z` bias of ~0.2–0.3 mm across the noisy
scenarios (clean-data recovery is 0.002 mm, so it comes from the one-sided
flying-pixel distribution, not from the estimator's geometry). It is inside the
reported σ and two orders below the errors this feature exists to remove.

## Open items

- **Depth bias.** ToF range bias is real and currently lands in `t_z`. Mostly
  separable (bias is range-dependent, `t_z` is not) but only identifiable across
  genuinely varied distances — which a passive system cannot demand. The
  information-gain criterion would extend to it naturally if added.
- **Joint-angle offsets.** Deliberately excluded: if per-link residuals disagree,
  that is information, not a nuisance parameter to absorb.
- **Multi-camera.** The estimator is per-camera; a shared robot pose across
  cameras is a strictly larger problem.
